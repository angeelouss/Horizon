### [OpenAI 据报推翻指数时间假设](https://www.techmeme.com/261107/p12#a261107p12) ⭐️ 9.0/10

据《纽约时报》记者 Cade Metz 报道，OpenAI 被报道推翻了计算复杂性理论中长期存在的指数时间假设（Exponential Time Hypothesis）。该假设认为某些问题始终难以被计算机快速求解，其共同提出者 Russell Impagliazzo 称这一结果为“令人震惊的消息”。报道称，这一结果未来可能加快芯片设计并影响加密技术，但摘要中将这些影响表述为可能性而非已实现的能力。由于提供的材料仅为简短摘要，证明细节和独立验证情况无法确认。

rss · Techmeme · Nov 7, 17:15

**「Background」** The Exponential Time Hypothesis, co-proposed by Russell Impagliazzo in 1998, holds that problems such as 3-SAT cannot be solved in subexponential time. If it is true, 3-SAT would have no polynomial-time algorithm, which would imply P ≠ NP. Horizon&\#x27;s November 1 digest reported that OpenAI had claimed its internal model contributed, in theory, to progress on this hypothesis, but had not published the results in bulk and was testing expert review of them. The reported 2^o\(n\) algorithms for 3-SAT, machine-checked in Lean, are the concrete result now being described.

**「Implications for Encryption」** The result does not by itself weaken current encryption, because disproving the Exponential Time Hypothesis is not a proof that P = NP and does not show that the specific hard problems used in practice have become easy. Security teams should treat the encryption and chip-design effects mentioned in the report as speculative long-term possibilities rather than near-term changes to deployed systems.

**「专家反应」** 目前可用的材料只包含 Impagliazzo 对这一结果的评价，即“令人震惊的消息”，并未提供更多专家讨论。

### [Censys finds 800+ publicly exposed Artex AI hacking tool deployments](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/06/hundreds-of-artex-ai-hacking-tools-found-publicly-exposed-to-the-web/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Censys identified more than 800 distinct deployments of Artex, an open-source autonomous penetration-testing tool built from AI agents, that were reachable from the web without a password. Security firms have linked Artex to attacks on South Korean finance firms in September, which CrowdStrike attributed to a Chinese-speaking hacker who left an open directory, and to an October intrusion that Microsoft said let hackers steal data from customers&\#x27; cloud environments at speed, with the broader campaign reportedly affecting thousands of organizations. Many of these attacks relied on weak or known passwords and missing multi-factor authentication, and Censys said the software does not require internet exposure, so the reason for the public instances is unclear. Artex has announced it will go closed-source, but dozens of copies of its code remain on code-sharing sites and can still be used in active attacks.

rss · TechCrunch - AI · Nov 6, 18:44

**「Why open-source AI pentesting tools are a double-edged risk」** Autonomous penetration-testing tools automatically probe systems for weaknesses, so security teams can use them for authorized testing while attackers can use the same capability. Artex was released publicly in July as free, open-source software that anyone can run on their own servers, which is why it appeals to actors who cannot access the most advanced commercial cybersecurity models from Anthropic or OpenAI.

**「Action for operators」** Teams that run Artex or similar self-hosted AI penetration-testing tools should confirm that their consoles are not reachable from the internet and that they require authentication, because Censys found more than 800 deployments accessible without a password, even though the software does not need public exposure. Because the framework is dual-use rather than a malware family, as Censys described it, defenders should also review their own environments for unauthorized instances and check whether attackers could reuse exposed tools against them.
